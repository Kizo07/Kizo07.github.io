Factor Modeling

From factor idea to verdict

Distribution checks. Concentration checks. Rank tests. Portfolio tests.

  • Start with the cross-section. A factor with a broken or wildly skewed distribution needs different treatment before it becomes portfolio logic.
  • Inspect concentration. Sector, industry, and market-cap breakdowns reveal whether the factor is actually measuring the claimed economic intuition.
  • Measure rank quality. IC, Spearman correlation, monotonicity, and top-minus-bottom spread tell a richer story than one return number.
  • Backtest buckets and the portfolio. Quintiles and deciles show whether the signal degrades smoothly or only works in the extremes.
  • End with a verdict. Users need to know whether the factor is promising, weak, noisy, sector-biased, or only conditionally useful.

Risk Design

Sector composition is not a footnote

Signal or sector bet. Separate them before trusting the spread.

  • Sector composition by quintile shows whether the top bucket is diversified or simply overexposed to one economic theme.
  • Median factor values by sector help separate stock-level signal from industry structure.
  • Industry-level breakdowns catch hidden clusters that broad sector labels can smooth over.
  • A benchmark line or universe median is useful because users need an anchor before judging whether a bar is extreme.

Derivatives

Numerical methods as product design

Method choice → visible risk surface.

  • Lattices are useful when early exercise and decision trees need to be visible.
  • Least Squares Monte Carlo is powerful when the exercise policy is estimated from simulated paths.
  • Finite difference methods make surfaces and Greeks easier to inspect across grids.
  • Variance reduction is not cosmetic. It can decide whether a Monte Carlo estimate is usable inside an interactive workflow.
  • For securitized products, rate scenarios and embedded optionality need to be reviewable together.
